Recruitment Leads: Where to Find Client Leads

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 6 min read
← Back to Blog

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW. Updated July 2026.

Recruitment leads, in the sense that grows an agency, are potential client companies, not candidates. This trips people up, so to be clear from the start: this guide is about finding companies that will pay you to fill their roles, not about finding people to place. Here is where those client leads actually come from, how to tell a good one from a bad one, and how to turn a list of companies into booked meetings.

Candidate Leads vs Client Leads

Recruiters swim in candidate data. Client leads are the harder and more valuable kind, because a client relationship produces many placements over time while a candidate produces one. When your agency's growth stalls, it is almost always a shortage of client leads, not candidate leads. So the sourcing effort that actually moves revenue is aimed at companies, not people.

Where Client Leads Come From

Companies actively hiring. The strongest signal a company needs a recruiter is that they are already trying to hire. Job boards, company careers pages, and hiring announcements are all sources of companies with live need. A company that just posted five roles is a warmer lead than one picked at random.

Funding and growth events. A company that raised money or opened a new location is about to hire. These events are public and they predict recruitment need before the roles are even posted.

Your niche's ecosystem. Industry news, associations, and events in your specialty surface companies that fit your focus. Narrow specialties make this easier, because the ecosystem is smaller and more identifiable.

Competitor dissatisfaction. Companies publicly frustrated with their current agency, a slow shortlist, a bad placement, are leads with active intent to switch.

Dormant past clients. Companies you worked with before hire again. Re-engaging them is often the highest-conversion source of all, because the relationship already exists.

What Makes a Good Recruitment Lead

Volume of leads matters less than fit. A good client lead is a company that hires the roles you specialise in, has the budget to use an agency, and shows a signal of current or upcoming need. A thousand random companies is a worse list than two hundred that match all three. This is why buying giant generic contact lists rarely works, they are wide and shallow, and recruitment buyers can tell when they are one of ten thousand.

How to Turn Leads Into Meetings

A lead is only potential until you reach it well. That means identifying every decision maker at the company rather than one, reaching them across email and LinkedIn, and following up past the point most agencies quit, since most replies come between the fifth and twelfth touch. The first line should reference why they are a lead, the role they posted, the round they raised, not your credentials. This is the engine covered in full in the guide on recruitment agency sales.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are recruitment leads?

In the sense that grows an agency, recruitment leads are potential client companies that will pay you to fill their roles. This is distinct from candidate leads, which are people to place. Client leads drive revenue because one client produces many placements.

Where do recruitment agencies get client leads?

From companies actively hiring, funding and growth events, your niche's industry ecosystem, companies publicly unhappy with their current agency, and dormant past clients. The strongest leads show a live or imminent hiring signal.

Is it better to buy recruitment leads or generate them?

Generating targeted leads that fit your niche and show hiring signals beats buying large generic lists, which are wide and shallow. Recruitment buyers can tell when they are one of thousands on a purchased list, and reply rates reflect it.

How do I turn recruitment leads into clients?

Reach every decision maker at the company across email and LinkedIn, follow up past the fifth-to-twelfth touch where replies cluster, and open with why they are a lead rather than with your credentials. Depth and relevance convert leads to meetings.
