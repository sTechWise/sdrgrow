How Many Touches Does It Take to Win a Recruitment Client?

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: Plan for 12 to 16 touches over 45 to 60 days, across email and LinkedIn. Most positive replies arrive between touch 5 and touch 12. Agencies that stop after two emails quit before the reply window opens. That is the main reason owners say outbound does not work.

Key takeaways

  • One or two emails is not a campaign. It is a coin flip.
  • Replies cluster in the middle of a sequence, not the start.
  • Two channels beat one. Email and LinkedIn reinforce each other.
  • Spacing matters. 16 touches in one week is spam. Over 60 days it is presence.

Why do short campaigns fail?

Trust needs repetition. A hiring manager sees your first email between forty other messages. Even a good message earns a mental note at best. The second touch reminds them you exist. By the fifth they recognize the name. Somewhere between touch 5 and 12 the timing lines up: a role opens, a contractor quits, a hire falls through. You are the name in their head at that moment. Stop at touch 2 and someone else gets that moment.

What goes into a 16 touch sequence?

Half the touches live on email, half on LinkedIn. The email side moves through stages: a personal opener, a value follow up, proof, a direct ask, a break up note, and a final value angle. The LinkedIn side runs beside it: a profile view, a connection request, a welcome message, comments on their posts, one direct pitch, one referral ask. Each channel makes the other feel familiar. This is the structure behind the SDR GROW 16 touch engine.

How do you space the touches?

Spread them across 60 days. Early touches sit closer together, two to four days apart. Later touches spread out to a week or more. The rhythm should feel like a person following up, not a machine on a timer. Pause the sequence the moment anyone replies.

Checklist: sequence quality

  • At least 12 touches planned.
  • Both email and LinkedIn included.
  • Each touch says something new. No "just bumping this."
  • One clear call to action per message.
  • Auto stop on reply.
  • First lines tied to the prospect's open roles.

Example

An agency targets a fintech scaling its engineering team. Touches 1 to 4 get silence. Touch 5, a case study, gets an open but no reply. Touch 8 lands the same week their senior hire resigns. The manager replies: "Good timing. Can we talk this week?" Nothing about the message changed. The sequence kept the agency present until timing did the work.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Quitting after two touches.
  • Sending "just following up" with no new value.
  • Cramming all touches into ten days.
  • Running email alone when your buyers live on LinkedIn.
  • Pitching in the LinkedIn connection request itself.

FAQ

Is 16 touches too many? Will people get annoyed??

Spread over 60 days across two channels, each touch is small. Annoyance comes from empty messages, not from count.

When should I give up on a prospect??

After the full sequence ends, move them to a long term list. Re-enter them when a trigger event fires, like new job postings.

Does this work for small agencies??

Yes. The sequence is the same. Only the list size changes.
