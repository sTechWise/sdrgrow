How to Win a Client Away From Another Agency, Fair and Square (2026)

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: Win incumbent held clients by being the visible, patient alternative until the incumbent's inevitable bad month, then converting on delivered proof rather than promises. The fair and square constraints are also the effective ones: never bash the incumbent, never induce contract breaches, enter as the second supplier rather than demanding replacement, and let your delivery do the displacing. Clients switched through tact and evidence stay; clients switched through discounts and drama shop again next quarter.

Key takeaways

  • Almost every good client is someone's client. Displacement is the market's main event.
  • The backup supplier position converts to primary on merit, without asking anyone to break anything.
  • Incumbent bashing loses even when accurate: it audits your future behavior in the prospect's mind.
  • The patient path is faster than it looks: incumbents fail on their own schedule, reliably.

How the switch looked from the client's side of the table.

Told as the talent director later told it. "The agency we used was fine, then gradually not fine, the way these things go. Around the time the shortlists started slowing, this other firm began appearing: an email about our open roles that actually named them correctly, a useful comment under my post about interview drop offs, a note when we announced the new office. Never pushy, never a word about our agency, which I noticed, because two other firms that contacted me that year opened by trashing whoever we were using, and I remember thinking: that will be us they trash next. When the big product hire stalled for seven weeks, I did not go looking for anyone. I emailed the firm that had been quietly around, asked if they would run it in parallel. They filled it in nineteen days. The next three roles went to them without a conversation. We never formally switched. It just became true." Every mechanism in the story is buildable: the role citing touches, the substantive comment, the event note, the restraint. The SDR GROW 16 touch flow and its signal feeds manufacture exactly this presence at scale, and the Brand Voice module holds the tone that made the deciding difference: the firm that never bashed was the firm that got the call.

What are the fair and square rules, precisely?

Four. No incumbent bashing, ever, including when the prospect starts it: mirror the pain, not the blame. No inducement to breach: if an exclusivity or retained agreement exists, the parallel offer waits or narrows to uncovered roles; deals built on broken contracts teach clients how yours will end. Enter as addition: the second supplier ask is small, prudent and almost always grantable; replacement demands force a loyalty referendum you lose. Displace by delivery: the trial role is the whole argument. Win it clean and the account migrates itself, the way the talent director described: it just becomes true.

Checklist: the displacement campaign

  • Incumbent held targets identified via rival boards and role patterns.
  • Long presence sequences running: this play is measured in months.
  • Switch warning signs monitored for the timing moment.
  • The parallel or overflow ask drafted, small and specific.
  • Zero rival references in any touch, verified before sending.
  • Trial role delivery treated as the pitch it is: your best consultant, fastest clock.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Opening with the incumbent's failures. The prospect hears their own future.
  • Discount led displacement. Price switched clients are price switched away.
  • Demanding exclusivity on entry, converting a low risk trial into a high risk decision.
  • Winning the trial and delivering incumbently. The comparison window never really closes.

FAQ

Is targeting a rival's known clients ethical at all??

It is the definition of a market. The ethics live in the methods: public information, honest positioning, no induced breaches, no bad mouthing.

What if the prospect asks directly what we think of their agency??

Decline the invitation gracefully: "They are a serious firm; markets this hard strain everyone. Here is how we handle that strain." The restraint is the answer.

How long does displacement typically take??

Months of presence until the incumbent's stumble, then weeks. The sequence's job is to make the timing irrelevant to you: whenever it comes, you are already there.
