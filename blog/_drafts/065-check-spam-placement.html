How to Check If Your Emails Land in Spam (2026)

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: Three methods, cheapest first: seed testing, sending to your own accounts across Gmail, Outlook and others and looking where mail lands. Placement testing tools, which automate seeds across dozens of inboxes and score you. And metric forensics, reading your open, reply and bounce trends for the signature of placement decay. Run the seed check weekly per sending domain. Placement problems announce themselves quietly for weeks before they announce themselves expensively.

Key takeaways

  • Open rate is a smoke alarm, not a diagnosis. Seeds are the diagnosis.
  • Check per domain and per inbox: placement problems are usually local at first.
  • Gmail and Outlook behave differently. Test both, always.
  • Weekly checks catch the two to three week window where fixes are cheap.

How does seed testing work in practice?

Create test accounts on the major providers: two Gmail, two Outlook, one or two others matching your market. Add them into a real campaign batch, not a special send, since providers can treat one off sends differently. Then look: primary inbox, promotions or other tabs, junk, or nothing at all. Repeat weekly per sending domain and log the results, because the trend is the signal: a domain drifting from primary to promotions to junk over three weeks is telling you a story while there is still time to change the ending.

What do the tools add over manual seeds?

Breadth and history. Placement testing services fan your message across dozens of seed inboxes spanning providers and regions, score the placement, flag which authentication checks passed, and chart your trend over months. For agencies running multiple domains, the automation earns its modest cost quickly. Inside SDR GROW, the email pipeline runs placement monitoring as standard and rotates a wobbling inbox out of the lattice before its wound spreads, which is placement checking upgraded from a report into a reflex.

How do you read the metrics forensically?

Placement decay has a signature. Opens slide first, gradually, while bounces stay flat, which distinguishes it from a list quality problem where bounces spike. Replies then fall out of proportion to the open decline, because spam folders get zero replies ever. Meanwhile clicks from the emails that do land stay normal, ruling out a message problem. When the pattern reads slide, silence, normal, the diagnosis is placement, and seeds confirm it in a day.

Checklist: placement monitoring routine

  • Seed accounts live on Gmail, Outlook and one market relevant other.
  • Seeds included in real batches weekly, per sending domain.
  • Results logged so trends are visible, not remembered.
  • Open, reply and bounce trends reviewed together monthly.
  • Any drift triggers the recovery playbook, not a volume push.

Example

An agency's Monday seed check shows their second domain landing in Outlook junk while Gmail holds primary. Investigation finds a DKIM key dropped during a weekend platform update, Outlook enforcing harder than Gmail as usual. Fixed by Tuesday, reputation barely scratched. Without the weekly seed, the first symptom would have been three silent weeks and a recovery month. The check took four minutes.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting open rates alone as placement truth while tracking pixels grow less reliable.
  • Testing with one off sends instead of real campaign batches.
  • Checking only Gmail because it is biggest. Outlook burns senders independently.
  • Running checks and never logging them, which hides the only thing that matters: the trend.

FAQ

How many seed addresses are enough??

Five or six across providers catches most problems. Tools scale it further when multiple domains justify it.

My seeds land fine but replies are still dead. What now??

Then placement is not the problem. Look at targeting, message and list relevance, in that order.

Do placement results differ by recipient company??

Corporate filters add a layer on top of provider filters, so enterprise heavy lists can underperform seeds. Seeds still catch the systemic problems, which are the fixable ones.
