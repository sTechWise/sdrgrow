How Do You Stop Recruitment Cold Emails Landing in Spam?

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: You stay out of spam with four things. Send from separate domains, not your main one. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before the first email. Warm each inbox for two to four weeks. Then keep volume steady and lists clean. Copy matters less than infrastructure. Most spam problems start before anyone writes a word.

Key takeaways

  • Never send cold email from your main domain.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC are required, not optional.
  • Warmup takes weeks. Plan for it.
  • High bounce rates burn domains faster than anything else.

Why do recruitment emails hit spam so often?

Recruitment is a crowded outbound niche. Inbox providers see heavy volume from the industry and filter hard. Agencies also make the classic errors: sending from the company's main domain, blasting cold lists on day one, and ignoring authentication records. Each error raises your spam score. Together they sink the campaign.

What do SPF, DKIM and DMARC do in plain English?

They prove you are who you say you are. SPF lists which servers may send for your domain. DKIM signs each email so nobody can tamper with it. DMARC tells inbox providers what to do when a message fails those checks. Without all three, Gmail and Outlook treat you as a stranger. Strangers go to spam.

How does domain warmup work?

A new domain has no reputation. Warmup builds one. You start with a few emails a day to inboxes that open and reply. Volume rises over two to four weeks. Only then does the domain carry real campaigns. Skip this and providers flag the sudden volume as spam behavior. SDR GROW sets up domains, authentication and warmup as part of onboarding, so agencies never learn this lesson the expensive way.

Checklist: deliverability before you scale

  • Separate sending domains, main domain untouched.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass on every domain.
  • Each inbox warmed for at least two weeks.
  • List verified, bounce risk under 3 percent.
  • Daily volume per inbox capped at a safe level.
  • A way to test where your emails land.

Example

An agency owner sends 500 emails from his main domain in one day. Half bounce or sit unopened. Within a week his normal client emails start landing in spam too. He now has a business problem, not a marketing problem. The fix took a month: new sending domains, proper records, slow warmup. The lesson costs nothing to learn in advance.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sending cold email from the domain your clients know.
  • Buying a list and sending the same day.
  • Ramping volume in jumps instead of steps.
  • Ignoring bounces until the domain is burned.
  • Using one inbox for everything instead of rotation.

FAQ

How many cold emails can one inbox send per day??

Keep it modest. Somewhere in the range of 30 to 50 per inbox per day is a common safe practice. Scale by adding inboxes, not by pushing one harder.

How long until a new domain can run full campaigns??

Two to four weeks of warmup for most setups.

Can I fix a burned domain??

Sometimes, with months of clean behavior. Buying a fresh sending domain is faster. Protect the main domain at all costs.
