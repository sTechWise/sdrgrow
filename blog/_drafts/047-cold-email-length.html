How Long Should a Cold Email Be? (2026)

By Abir Hossain · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: Under 100 words for the first touch, and 50 to 80 is the sweet spot. Later touches can run slightly longer when they carry real substance, and the break up email should be shortest of all, under 60. The rule behind the numbers: a cold email must be readable in full on a phone screen without scrolling, in the ten seconds a stranger's message gets. Length is not a style choice. It is a survival constraint.

Key takeaways

  • Phones set the limit: one screen, no scroll, or the bottom half never existed.
  • Every sentence must fight for its place. Most drafts carry passengers.
  • Short signals confidence. Long signals a pitch deck wearing an email costume.
  • The 100 word budget forces the choices that make emails good.

Why does short win with strangers?

Attention economics. A known sender earns reading time. A stranger gets a glance, and the glance decides. Long emails fail the glance twice: they look like work before a word is read, and their ask drowns below the fold. Short emails also flatter the reader: brevity says you value their time, essays say you value your services. In an inbox of forty daily pitches, the 60 word email is the one that looks answerable.

Where do the words go in an 80 word budget?

Roughly: 20 words for the signal opener, 15 for who you are, 20 for proof, 10 for the ask, the rest for connective tissue and sign off. Nothing else fits, which is the point: no history paragraph, no service menus, no second proof point, no attachments announced. The discipline is baked into the SDR GROW 16 touch flow's email templates, where each slot has a word budget and Brand Voice trims drafts that swell, because the system's job includes protecting sends from the human urge to add one more sentence.

When can emails run longer?

Mid sequence value touches carrying a genuine insight, a market breakdown, a candidate availability note, can stretch to 120 words if the substance holds. Replies to interested prospects leave cold email rules entirely: once they answer, write like the relationship it now is. The rule bends on substance, never on enthusiasm.

Checklist: length discipline

  • First touch under 100 words, counted, not guessed.
  • Full email visible on a phone without scrolling.
  • Zero paragraphs about agency history.
  • Each sentence removable test: delete it, if nothing breaks, it was a passenger.
  • Longer touches justified by substance, listed explicitly.

Example

Draft one: 240 words covering agency background, service range, two case studies, a market observation and three contact options. Draft two, same sender, same prospect: "The five week old data lead role caught my eye. We placed two identical roles in your market this quarter, both inside four weeks. Worth 15 minutes Thursday?" Thirty six words. Draft two got the meeting, and in it, the prospect said the magic sentence: "Yours was the only recruiter email I actually read."

Mistakes to avoid

  • Explaining everything in touch one. The sequence has fifteen more chances.
  • Bullet lists in cold emails. Structure formality reads as brochure.
  • Cutting the proof to save words. Cut the self description instead.
  • Padding short emails back up with pleasantries. Short plus hollow is worse than long.

FAQ

Do longer emails ever outperform??

To warm audiences and newsletter subscribers, yes. To strangers, the data has pointed one direction for years.

Does the subject line count in the budget??

Separately: two to four plain words. "sales roles" outperforms clever every week.

What about email signatures??

Name, firm, one line, phone. Banner images and inspirational quotes cost deliverability and credibility together.
