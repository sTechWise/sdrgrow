How to Find Clients for a Staffing Agency

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 6 min read
← Back to Blog

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW. Updated July 2026.

Finding clients is the constant pressure of running a staffing agency. You can be excellent at placing people and still struggle, because filling roles and finding the companies that give you roles are different jobs. This guide is a practical walkthrough of how to find staffing clients: where they are, how to reach them, and how to keep a steady flow instead of lurching between busy and empty.

Why Finding Staffing Clients Is Hard

Two reasons. First, the companies you want are pitched by staffing agencies constantly, so standing out is harder than it used to be. Second, most agency owners find clients reactively, chasing hard when work is slow and stopping when it picks up, which guarantees the slow periods keep coming back. The agencies with steady client flow have made finding clients a continuous process rather than an emergency response.

Where to Find Staffing Clients

Companies with open roles right now. The clearest sign a company needs staffing help is that they are already hiring. Roles posted on job boards and careers pages point you straight at companies with live need in your specialty.

Companies growing or funded. A funding round or expansion means hiring is coming. Reaching these companies early, before they are flooded with pitches, wins the relationship.

Your specialty's industry channels. Trade associations, industry news, and events in your niche surface the companies that fit you. A narrow focus makes this far easier because the field is smaller.

Companies unhappy with their current provider. Businesses publicly frustrated with a slow or low-quality staffing partner are ready to switch and worth reaching directly.

Former clients. Companies you placed for before will hire again. Systematic re-engagement of past clients is often the fastest source of new work.

How to Reach Staffing Clients So They Respond

Finding the company is half the job. Reaching them well is the other half. Identify every decision maker who touches hiring, the hiring manager, the HR lead, the operations lead who owns headcount, rather than emailing one person and hoping. Reach them across email and LinkedIn together, and follow up past where most agencies quit, because most replies land between the fifth and twelfth touch. Open with something about them, a role they posted, a location they opened, not with how established your agency is. The complete system behind this is in the guide on staffing agency marketing.

How to Keep Finding Clients Consistently

The reason most staffing agencies swing between feast and famine is that client-finding stops the moment they get busy delivering. The work you need in two months comes from the outreach you do now, so continuous outreach, running whether or not you have time this week, is what flattens the swings. This is the single hardest discipline to sustain by hand, and it is why systematising it changes an agency's trajectory.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do staffing agencies find clients?

By targeting companies with live hiring need, companies that are growing or funded, their specialty's industry channels, companies unhappy with a current provider, and former clients. The strongest prospects show a current or imminent hiring signal.

What is the fastest way to find staffing clients?

Re-engaging former clients and reaching companies with roles posted right now are the fastest, because the need is already there. New outreach to a targeted list produces first meetings in weeks four to six once infrastructure is warmed.

How do new staffing agencies get their first clients?

Pick a narrow specialty, build a list of companies hiring in it now, and run a full-depth outreach sequence reaching every decision maker. A narrow niche with live hiring signals beats a broad market for landing the first clients.

Why do staffing agencies struggle to find clients?

Usually because client-finding is reactive, stopping when the agency gets busy and restarting in a panic when work slows. Continuous outreach that runs during busy months is what produces steady client flow.
