How Do Recruitment Agencies Win Google Traffic From Competitor Content?

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Direct answer: Find the blog posts sending traffic to your competitors, then publish a better page on the same topic. Better means more complete, more current, better structured, and backed by your own experience. Google and AI search reward the strongest page on a topic, not the first one. Most agency competitors wrote their posts years ago and never updated them. That is your opening.

Key takeaways

  • Your competitors already proved which topics bring traffic. Use that proof.
  • You compete by writing the better page, not a copy.
  • Structure matters: direct answers, headings as questions, FAQ sections.
  • Updated content beats stale content on the same topic.

How do you find what drives competitor traffic?

Every agency with a blog has a handful of pages doing all the work. SEO tools reveal them: which pages rank, for which questions, with how many backlinks. Track up to ten competitors and a pattern appears fast. The same topics come up: salary guides, hiring trend posts, interview question lists, cost of a bad hire. Demand is proven. The SDR GROW blog analysis module surfaces the top traffic page per competitor each week, with word count and backlink data, so you know what you are up against.

What makes your version win?

Four things beat an old ranking page. Depth: cover what, why, how, risks and fixes, where the old page covers only what. Freshness: 2026 data against their 2022 data. Structure: direct answer at the top, question headings, a checklist and FAQ that AI search can lift. Experience: your placement stories and your market numbers, which no competitor can copy. Write for the reader first. Rankings follow usefulness.

Should you ever copy the competitor's post?

No. Copied content risks a duplicate content problem with Google and a reputation problem with peers. It also caps you at their level. The goal is the better page, and a copy is never the better page. Treat their post as a brief that proves demand and shows the bar to clear.

Checklist: before you publish a competing page

  • The topic drives real traffic for at least one competitor.
  • Your page answers the question in the first paragraph.
  • Headings are questions people ask.
  • You added something only you have: data, stories, opinion.
  • FAQ section included with schema markup.
  • Publish date and author visible.
  • Two internal links added.

Example

A rival agency ranks for "how much does a bad hire cost." Their post is 800 words from 2021. You publish 1,500 words with 2026 numbers, a cost checklist, three real anonymized examples from your placements, and an FAQ. Six weeks later your page sits above theirs and AI assistants cite yours. Same topic. Better page.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Copying instead of outdoing.
  • Chasing topics with no proven traffic.
  • Publishing thin AI text with no real input from you.
  • Writing the page and never updating it.
  • Skipping schema and structure, which AI search relies on.

FAQ

How many competitor topics should I target??

Start with five. One good page a week beats twenty rushed ones.

How long until a page ranks??

Weeks for low competition topics, months for hard ones. Recruitment niches skew low competition.

Does this help with ChatGPT and AI search too??

Yes. AI answers pull from the strongest ranking pages. Win the topic and you enter the answers.
