How Do Recruitment Agencies Get New Clients?

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 7 min read
← Back to Blog

Every recruitment agency gets clients from some mix of seven channels. Most agencies use two of them, referrals and job board scraping, and hit a ceiling. This page ranks all seven by how controllable and scalable each one actually is, with the honest costs and timelines, so you can decide where your next client actually comes from instead of waiting for the phone.

1. Referrals and Repeat Business

The best clients you will ever sign. Warm, pre-sold, cheap. Also completely uncontrollable: you cannot schedule one, forecast on them, or scale them past your network's size. Agencies have a 60 to 70 percent chance of winning business from past clients even years later, so work the base you have. Ask after every successful placement, not once a year. But treat referrals as the bonus channel, not the plan. Every agency stuck at the same revenue for three years is an agency whose plan was referrals.

2. Structured Outbound

Email and LinkedIn sequences to a defined list of target companies. Ranked second because it is the most controllable channel on this list: you choose exactly who to contact, when, and at what volume, and it scales by turning a dial rather than hiring.

What separates outbound that works from the outbound everyone complains about is depth and coverage. Most replies arrive between touch 5 and touch 12, so two-email attempts fail by design. And emailing one contact per company is a coin flip, because that one person is busy or not the buyer. The working shape: every relevant layer of the company, founder to founder and manager to manager, 12 to 16 touches over 45 to 60 days, both channels together.

Cost: DIY stack roughly $1,500 a month plus 100+ hours of labour. Done-for-you systems $1,200 to $3,500 a month. An in-house SDR $4,000 to $6,000 before tools. First meetings in weeks four to six once domains are warmed. Disclosure: this channel is what SDR GROW does, so weigh my ranking accordingly, then check the reasoning against your own experience of the other six.

3. Job Change and Hiring Triggers

Reaching out when something just happened: the company posted five new roles, raised a round, opened an office, or a contact you placed years ago just became a hiring manager somewhere new. Trigger-based outreach converts several times better than cold contact because the need is live right now. On its own it is a tactic. Fed into your outbound sequences as the reason for the first line, it is the difference between "we are a leading agency" and a message about their actual open roles. That second message gets read.

4. LinkedIn Presence

Not automation. Presence: posting consistently in your niche so hiring managers recognise your name before any outreach lands. This channel compounds with outbound rather than competing with it, because a prospect who has seen your posts replies to your email at a visibly higher rate than a cold one.

The realistic version for a busy founder: study the five recruiters in your niche who already get engagement, note which of their posts work, write your own take on those themes, twenty minutes a day. Expect three to six months before inbound enquiries start. Slow to start, durable once running.

5. Content and SEO

Salary guides, hiring reports, niche market breakdowns that rank on Google and bring hiring managers to you. Highest trust of any channel, because the client found you. Also the slowest: six to twelve months before meaningful traffic on a new domain, and it rewards consistency most agencies cannot sustain. Worth doing in parallel with faster channels, never as the only bet. The shortcut that works: find the one blog post driving your competitor's search traffic, and write the better version of that exact page.

6. Partnerships

Adjacent service providers who talk to your buyers before you do: HR consultants, employment lawyers, accountants who see hiring plans, complementary agencies covering roles you do not. A steady partnership can feed deals for years. It takes months of relationship building, produces unpredictably, and works best as a background channel, ten percent of effort for occasional high-quality deals.

7. Paid Ads

Ranked last for most agencies deliberately. Recruitment services are a considered, relationship purchase, and cold traffic from a Google ad rarely signs a retainer. Cost per qualified lead commonly lands at $150 to $400, and the leads still need the same nurture as outbound contacts. The exception where paid works: retargeting people who already visited your site, and promoting a genuinely good content asset in a tight niche. As a primary acquisition channel for an agency under $2M, it mostly burns budget.

The Mix That Actually Works

For an agency past referral ceiling and under roughly $2M: structured outbound as the primary controllable channel, triggers feeding it, LinkedIn presence compounding it, one strong content asset per quarter, referral asks systematised after every placement. That mix has one channel you fully control, two that make it convert better, and two slow ones building in the background. Ads only once retargeting has an audience to work with.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest way for a recruitment agency to get new clients?

Trigger-based outbound to companies actively hiring right now, run as a proper multichannel sequence. Warmed and running, first meetings typically arrive in weeks four to six. Everything faster than that is either a referral or luck.

How much should a recruitment agency spend on client acquisition?

A workable outbound-led mix runs $1,200 to $3,500 a month systematised, or $1,500 a month in tools plus half a person's time if built in-house. Compare against one placement fee to judge return: for most agencies a single signed client pays for six to twelve months of the entire channel.

Do cold emails still work for recruitment agencies in 2026?

Single generic emails, effectively no: 1 to 2 percent reply rates and falling. Multichannel sequences of 12 to 16 touches with trigger-based first lines targeting every layer of the company still work, aiming at 8 to 15 percent replies. The channel did not die. The two-email template version of it did.

How do new recruitment agencies with no network get their first clients?

Outbound is usually the only controllable option, because referrals need a base and SEO needs a year. Pick a tight niche, build a list of companies hiring in it right now, and run a full-depth sequence. A narrow niche with live hiring triggers beats a broad market every time for a first client.
