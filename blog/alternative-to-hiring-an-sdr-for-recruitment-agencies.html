Alternative to Hiring an SDR for Recruitment Agencies

By Abir, Founder of SDR GROW · July 2026 · 7 min read
← Back to Blog

You are here because the math on an SDR hire made you flinch. Fair. A junior SDR runs $4,000 to $6,000 a month before tools, takes three to six months to reach full productivity, and in the meantime you are paying full salary to find out whether your outbound even works. If the hire quits at month seven, which in this role happens constantly, the process leaves with them and you start over. There are four real alternatives. Here is each one honestly, including where it fails.

Why the SDR Hire Is Usually the Wrong First Move

Not because SDRs are bad. A good one brings judgement no software has: reading a call, handling an objection live, picking up the phone. The problem is sequencing. Hiring a person before a proven process exists means paying them to invent the process, on your payroll, with no benchmark for whether their targeting or copy is any good. If results are poor, you cannot tell if the problem is the rep, the list, the message, or the market. That ambiguity costs you six months and thirty grand.

The agencies that make SDR hires work do it in the other order: prove the system first, then hire someone to operate and extend it. The hire steps into a machine with known numbers, and you can actually measure them.

Alternative 1: An Outbound System One Person Runs

The infrastructure of an SDR desk without the desk. Lead sourcing, verified contacts, sending domains, warming, a multichannel sequence, all configured, with one existing person on your team operating it from a dashboard for a few hours a month.

Cost: $1,200 to $3,500 a month for the vertical systems, usually plus setup. SDR GROW is ours, built only for recruitment agencies: $1,997 setup, $1,200 a month, 16 touches over 60 days across email and LinkedIn, reaching every layer of the target company rather than one contact.

Where it wins: running in days instead of quarters, costs a third of a hire, and the process belongs to you permanently. Nobody resigns and takes it.

Where it fails: it will not pick up a phone, and it cannot rescue a weak offer. If nobody has ever bought your service through any channel, fix positioning before buying any system.

Alternative 2: Outsourced SDR Agencies

A rep or pod at an agency works your account: their people, their tools, your targets.

Cost: $4,000 to $10,000 a month at the tier that actually works. Cheaper offshore versions exist at $500 to $1,500 and run template blasts, which fail specifically hard on recruitment buyers because those buyers receive more cold email than almost anyone in B2B and delete templates on sight.

Where it wins: genuine human touch at scale, including calling, without you managing headcount.

Where it fails: the good tier costs the same as the hire you were avoiding, your account is one of many, and the rep working it does not know recruitment. Generic SDRs pitching staffing services to HR directors is an awkward fit you can hear in the copy.

Alternative 3: The DIY Tool Stack

Assemble it yourself: a data tool, enrichment, a sender with warming, LinkedIn tooling, monitoring.

Cost: roughly $1,500 a month in software at the tiers that handle real volume. For the full cost breakdown, the honest cost is the labour: over 100 hours a month across list building, sequence writing, deliverability management, content and monitoring. That is more than half a full time person, forever.

Where it wins: total control, and genuinely the cheapest option if you already have an operator who enjoys building stacks and has the hours.

Where it fails: almost every agency that tries it. The stack gets built in an enthusiastic week, runs for three, then billing gets busy and it dies quietly. The tools were never the constraint. The hours were.

Alternative 4: A VA Running a System

The hybrid: a trained virtual assistant executes daily inside a configured system. Human judgement on replies at a fraction of SDR cost.

Cost: $1,000 to $2,500 a month for the VA on top of whatever the system costs. We offer this as an add-on at $2,000 a month for agencies that want zero hands on it.

Where it wins: fully hands-off for less than half an SDR, and the VA works inside a proven process instead of inventing one.

Where it fails: a VA without a system underneath is just a cheaper person doing unstructured outreach, and produces the same nothing. The system has to exist first.

Which One Fits You

Under $1M revenue, no spare operator: the one-person system. Lowest cost that actually works on recruitment buyers.

Growing, founder has no time for even a dashboard: system plus VA. Fully hands-off at roughly half an SDR.

Established, proven outbound already booking meetings, need more capacity: now hire the SDR. This is the point where the hire makes sense, because they operate a machine instead of inventing one.

Have a tools person with 100 spare hours a month: DIY honestly can work. Count the hours first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest alternative to hiring an SDR?

A vertical outbound system run by one existing person, at $1,200 to $3,500 a month against $4,000 to $6,000 for a hire before tools. The DIY stack looks cheaper on subscriptions alone but costs over 100 hours a month in labour, which makes it the most expensive option for most agencies once time is priced.

Can software fully replace an SDR?

It replaces the repeatable 80 percent: sourcing, sequencing, follow-up, monitoring. It does not replace calling or live objection handling. Most agencies find the 80 percent was the part they could never sustain, and the remaining 20 percent fits into the founder's existing week.

When should a recruitment agency actually hire an SDR?

Once outbound already works and is documented: known reply rates, proven sequences, steady meetings. The hire then adds capacity to a working machine. Hiring before that point means paying a salary to run an experiment.

How fast do the alternatives start producing meetings?

A configured system needs two to three weeks of domain warming, first replies in weeks three to four, steady meetings from week six. An SDR hire typically takes three to six months to reach the same point.
